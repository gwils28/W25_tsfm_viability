When does a zero-shot TSFM beat a refitted specialist?

W25 · French hourly load, 50 European load series, Chronos-Bolt and Chronos-2 against statsforecast

The plan of the week (a French brief, ISO week 25 of 2026) asks one question: when does a time series foundation model beat a supervised specialist, and when does it lose? And how do we turn that decision into a production component? It asks for the answer in one sentence, backed by interval coverage and not only by a mean error.

The answers

TipBlock 2, the sentence the plan asks for

On French hourly load in 2019, Chronos-Bolt base beats AutoETS at every horizon step and on every ordinary day type (MASE ratio 0.24, or 0.40 against an ETS with forced seasonality), with a 90% coverage of 0.86. That interval is its 80% one by construction. It under-covers on h 13-18 (0.79). On public holidays it is no better than the seasonal ETS and covers 37% of hours: zero-shot, it does not know the calendar. Told the calendar (a holiday flag as a known covariate, exploratory), Chronos-2 halves its holiday error and its yearly 90% coverage becomes 0.899.

TipBlock 3, the router

On 50 load series, Chronos-2 wins on all 200 (series, history) units, and most in cold start. The paper’s router thresholds lose accuracy out of sample. A router can only trade accuracy for a cost saving, and the measured cost of the specialist (a daily refit) is close to the TSFM’s. Its decision drifts: 40 of 50 series change branch over a year.

What the checks changed

What I expected or what the sources said What the measurement says
SeasonalNaive(24) is a strawman on load It beats the weekly naive on 5 weekdays of 7
AutoETS is a strong reference It picks a level-only model at 572 of 608 origins (AICc is one-step) and forecasts a flat line
Bolt’s 90% interval covers 0.86, inside the band It is the 80% interval: cov80 = cov90 = 0.858, in the band by accident
A well-calibrated interval is a good interval The flat AutoETS is the best-calibrated model (0.899) and the least useful (Winkler)
6 origins (the plan’s minimum) are enough Against MSTL, 24% of 6-origin draws reverse the ranking
The router saves 70% of FM cost (paper) That figure is an oracle; the deployed rule saves 33% (paper’s repo). Here no unit favours the specialist
An FM call costs 1000 specialist calls (paper) 95 ms (4 threads) vs 183 ms (1 core) for an MSTL refit
The GIFT-Eval leader has 18.5M parameters True in April 2026 under two filters; #13 in October

Method

  • Pre-registered protocols (block 2, block 3), committed before the first forecast. The one deviation (a seasonal ETS reference) is labelled wherever it appears.
  • Leakage tests: every refit and every context ends at the cutoff; future values are corrupted in the tests to prove it. Chronos-2’s cross_learning is pinned off.
  • Uncertainty over origins (moving blocks of 7 days) and over countries (block 3), never over hours.
  • Reproducible: bit-identical forecasts run to run; CPU vs GPU within 0.016 MW.
